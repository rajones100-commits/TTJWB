When do young people leave sport?

Exploring the drop-off in youth participation, the barriers behind it and the changes that could help more young people stay involved.

Explore the evidence ↓Read the sources ↗

Focus: ages 16–18. Sources use different age groups, which are labelled throughout.

01 / Know the terms

What counts as a drop-off?

A young person can leave a club yet remain active in other ways. These terms help us ask the right question.

Participation

Taking part

Doing sport or physical activity at a particular time. Surveys may measure club sport, informal sport or all physical activity differently.

Drop-off

Taking part less often

A reduction in how frequently someone participates. Comparing different age groups can suggest a pattern, but cannot show that the same people reduced their activity.

Dropout

Stopping an activity

Leaving a specific sport, club or programme. This does not automatically mean someone has stopped all sport or exercise.

Reading the numbers correctly

Lower participation among older pupils is evidence of an age-related pattern. Measuring how many individuals actually stop requires following the same people over time or asking about their past and current participation.

03 / The barriers

Why might participation change?

Research points to several overlapping reasons. They affect different young people in different ways.

01 / ACCESS

Cost and transport

Fees, equipment and travel can make opportunities harder to reach. Gwent peer research also emphasised safe, local venues. StreetGames · Gwent research

02 / TIME

Other demands

Study, work, other interests and the commitment required by formal sessions can compete with sport. StreetGames · Gwent research

03 / EXPERIENCE

Confidence and belonging

Confidence, body image and feeling comfortable in a session matter, particularly in research about teenage girls. Friends can make trying an activity easier. Women in Sport · Youth Sport Trust · Gwent research

04 / CHOICE

One format does not suit everyone

Some young people value competition; others want informal play, different sports or more freedom to choose. Gwent research

04 / Why it matters

What could be lost?

Sport can provide movement, friendship, enjoyment and a sense of achievement. Leaving a club does not necessarily mean losing all of those benefits; the question is whether a young person finds another way to be active.

Physical health

Less opportunity to move

If overall activity falls, it may be harder to reach the UK Chief Medical Officers’ guidance for ages 5–18: an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day across the week. Read guidance ↗

Wellbeing

Fewer positive experiences

Sport and activity can support wellbeing and confidence. The effect of leaving will depend on the person and what activities replace it. Read guidance ↗

Connection

Less time with a sporting community

Young people in Gwent described friendships and belonging as valuable parts of sport. Leaving a team can mean losing that particular social space. Read research ↗

05 / Responses

What could help young people stay?

These are practical options drawn from the barriers and preferences in the research. They are ideas to test locally, not proven fixes for every young person.

Option 1

Make it easier to get there

Use accessible local venues and consider the combined cost of sessions, kit and travel.

Option 2

Offer more than one way to play

Keep competitive pathways while adding informal, social or flexible sessions.

Option 3

Ask young people first

Find out which times, activities and session styles they would actually choose; involve them in shaping the offer.

Question to investigate: “If you played sport at 14–15, do you still take part now? If your participation changed, what made the biggest difference?” Ask separately about club sport, informal sport and other physical activity.
06 / What is being done

UK and Welsh action

Sport Wales

Uses the School Sport Survey to understand young people’s experiences and help organisations plan opportunities. Its strategy calls for flexible opportunities through life transitions. Evidence ↗ · Strategy ↗

StreetGames in Wales

Has worked with young peer researchers in Gwent and on the Us Girls Wales project to listen to young people and shape local activities around their preferences. Gwent ↗ · Us Girls Wales ↗

Women in Sport and Youth Sport Trust

Publish research on girls’ experiences, confidence and barriers, giving schools and providers evidence to consider when designing sessions. Women in Sport ↗ · Youth Sport Trust ↗
